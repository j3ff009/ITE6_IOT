01 Physical world to useful action

Sense, process, communicate, act

The Internet of Things is a network of physical objects with sensing, processing, software, and communication capabilities. Devices observe conditions, exchange data, and may trigger an automated response.

Sense

A device measures a physical condition.

Process

Firmware or software interprets the reading.

Communicate

Data travels through a network and protocol.

Act

A person or actuator changes the environment.

Smart server room: A temperature sensor sends readings through an ESP32 and Wi-Fi. A platform stores and evaluates the data, alerts staff, and can activate cooling through a relay.

02 Core components

Every block has a responsibility

INPUT

Sensor

Measures temperature, humidity, light, motion, position, images, or another physical condition.

OUTPUT

Actuator

Performs an action through a relay, motor, valve, pump, buzzer, or smart lock.

LOGIC

Controller

Runs local logic and connects sensors and actuators, such as an ESP32, PLC, or Raspberry Pi.

LINK

Network

Transfers telemetry and commands using Wi-Fi, Ethernet, BLE, cellular, or LoRaWAN.

BRIDGE

Gateway / edge

Aggregates, translates, filters, secures, buffers, and locally processes device traffic.

VALUE

Platform and app

Ingests, stores, analyzes, manages devices, and presents dashboards, APIs, or business functions.

A sensor observes the environment; an actuator changes it. Device identity, configuration, credentials, firmware, network interface, and power are also essential.

03 Organized system design

Architecture is more than a diagram

Produce

Where is data created, and in what physical context?

Travel

Which links, protocols, addresses, and gateways carry it?

Process

Does work occur on the device, at the edge, in the cloud, or across all three?

Store

Is data kept locally, in a time-series database, or in a data lake?

Use

Which rules, analytics, dashboards, APIs, and workflows consume it?

Protect and manage

How are identity, encryption, access, updates, and health monitoring handled?

A sound design also satisfies performance, security, scalability, reliability, interoperability, manageability, cost, privacy, and legal requirements.

04 Basic academic model

Perception, network, application

P

Perception

Interfaces with the physical world through sensors, actuators, RFID, cameras, and microcontrollers.

N

Network

Moves data between devices, gateways, platforms, and applications.

A

Application

Turns data into user-facing or business value through apps, dashboards, and services.

Direction matters: Telemetry generally moves upward; commands and configuration move downward.

05 Expanded responsibilities

Separate transport, processing, and policy

1Perception

Collect measurements and perform physical actions.

2Transport

Move telemetry and commands across networks.

3Processing

Ingest, filter, store, analyze, and manage devices.

4Application

Provide domain services, interfaces, alerts, and APIs.

5Business

Define policy, KPIs, workflows, compliance, and decisions.

The five-layer model is useful for real systems because it separates connectivity from data handling and organizational value.

06 Production functional blocks

Devices to apps, with management across the fleet

1Devices

Sense, encode, and act.

2Gateway / edge

Aggregate, translate, filter, buffer, and decide locally.

3Message ingestion

Authenticate and receive telemetry.

4Storage and analytics

Retain history and extract insight.

5Apps and APIs

Inform people and business systems.

Device-management plane

Handles enrollment, identity, configuration, health, firmware updates, credential rotation, and retirement.

Cross-cutting security

Protects every block instead of appearing as a single isolated layer.

07 Processing placement

Put each decision where it fits best

Gateway

Connects constrained or local devices to wider services and can translate protocols, buffer outages, enforce security, and run rules.

Edge

Processes near the source for fast response, lower bandwidth, privacy, and continued local operation.

Cloud

Provides elastic storage, fleet management, large-scale analytics, integration, remote access, and organization-wide dashboards.

Common pattern: Use the edge for immediate decisions and resilience; use the cloud for fleet coordination, long-term history, and intensive analysis.

7.2 Design comparison

Placement follows requirements

DesignBest suited forStrengthsTrade-offs
Cloud-centeredNoncritical monitoring with stable internetCentral management and scalable analyticsHigher latency and connectivity dependence
Edge-centeredReal-time control or privacy-sensitive dataFast response, less bandwidth, offline operationMore local hardware and maintenance
HybridMost enterprise and industrial systemsLocal autonomy with central insightIntegration and synchronization complexity

A campus camera can count occupancy at the edge and send only totals to the cloud, reducing video traffic and privacy exposure while preserving building-wide reporting.

09 Decoupled messaging

Publishers and subscribers meet at a broker

Publisher

Sends a payload to a named topic.

Broker

Authenticates clients and routes messages.

Subscriber

Registers interest in one or more topics.

Topic and QoS

Organize messages hierarchically and select a delivery level.

Example topic: campus/buildingA/room201/temperature. MQTT still needs secure transport, authentication, authorization, careful topic design, and payload validation.

11 Comfort, energy, and resilience

One architecture, both data directions

1Room sensors

Temperature, CO2, motion, doors, and power.

2Edge gateway

Validate, add context, buffer, and enforce urgent thresholds.

3IoT platform

Store readings, analyze trends, and manage devices.

4Web app

Show rooms, alerts, history, and health.

5Facilities action

Apply energy policy or control selected loads.

Normal path

The gateway validates a timestamped room reading, adds building context, and forwards it. The cloud stores it and updates the dashboard; a high CO2 trend creates an alert.

Internet loss

The gateway continues urgent local rules and buffers readings. After reconnection, queued records retain their original timestamps.

12 Cross-layer responsibility

Protect devices, data, commands, and people

  • Unique device identity; no shared default passwords.
  • Secure boot, verified firmware, signed updates, rollback protection.
  • Encryption in transit and managed certificates or keys.
  • Least-privilege access for users, devices, services, and topics.
  • Minimal collection, protected storage, and defined retention.
  • Health, login, configuration, and anomaly monitoring.
  • Physical protection and controlled hardware access.
  • Secure provisioning, patching, credential rotation, and retirement.

A stolen credential can enable false telemetry or unauthorized commands. Mutual authentication, topic-level authorization, anomaly detection, and revocation reduce the risk.

12.2 Responsible data use

Collect less, separate identity, limit access

Purpose

Collect only what the stated function requires.

Separation

Separate identity from telemetry where possible.

Control

Restrict access and document retention.

Transparency

Inform affected users about processing.

Camera, location, health, and occupancy data deserve special care because they can reveal sensitive patterns about individuals.

13 Architecture trade-offs

Requirements shape placement and technology

GROW

Scalability

Partition topics and data, automate provisioning, and scale ingestion.

FAIL

Reliability and availability

Retry safely, buffer locally, remove single points of failure, add redundancy, backups, and recovery.

FAST

Latency

Place urgent logic on the device or edge and prioritize traffic.

SHARE

Interoperability

Use open protocols, stable APIs, schemas, units, and semantic models.

TRUST

Power and data quality

Plan sleep, sampling, batteries, calibration, timestamps, validation, and missing data.

OWN

Maintainability and cost

Support diagnostics, inventory, staged updates, ownership, connectivity, replacement, and staff.

Sending every raw camera frame to the cloud may simplify centralized analysis but increases bandwidth, cost, latency, and privacy risk.

15 Apply the design process

Build and justify a complete IoT architecture

Choose smart irrigation, cold-storage monitoring, campus energy management, flood warning, or vehicle tracking.

  1. State the goal and intended users.
  2. Name at least three sensors and one actuator.
  3. Select a controller, communication technology, and application protocol.
  4. Place processing on the device, edge, or cloud.
  5. Show telemetry and command directions.
  6. Describe normal operation and internet-loss behavior.
  7. Identify at least four security controls.
  8. Justify latency, reliability, scalability, interoperability, power, and cost.

Excellent evidence: Complete components and directions, technically suitable choices, clear flow, credible resilience and security, and explicit requirement-to-trade-off reasoning.

✓ Key takeaways

Connect the physical world to useful action

  • The three-layer model uses perception, network, and application.
  • The five-layer model adds transport, processing, and business responsibilities.
  • Telemetry moves upward; commands and configuration move downward.
  • Gateways aggregate, translate, secure, buffer, and run local rules.
  • Edge improves response, bandwidth, privacy, and resilience.
  • Cloud supports scale, history, fleet management, and wider analysis.
  • MQTT uses publishers, topics, a broker, subscribers, and QoS.
  • Security and system-design trade-offs shape every layer.
